A Simple Guide to Your Brain Activity

This report is a personalized map of your brain. Our goal is to see how your brain works and what makes it unique.

Introduction and Methods

The data encompasses an analysis of multiple EEG frequency bands collected from up to 32 scalp regions. The client's brain activity was averaged across several distinct logical and emotional tasks.

Tasks Overview

Task Description
MeditationThe client meditates with eyes closed to establish a baseline resting state.
MathsThe client solves math problems, assessing analytical reasoning and cognitive load management.
ValuesThe client labels phrases with "positive or negative signs", evaluating emotional processing and decision-making.
Word PairsThe client forms a sentence from two given words, testing verbal creativity and associative thinking.
Foot Trace & Ball TossThe client writes their name with their feet and plays catch, assessing motor planning, coordination, and sensory integration.
MusicThe client listens to music to observe responses to auditory and emotional stimuli.
PhoneThe client replies to messages on their phone, simulating real-world multitasking and social-cognitive engagement.
PictureThe client describes a picture and its emotional content, assessing visual processing, language expression, and social cognition.
GeometryThe client solves a geometry puzzle by mentally reshaping 3D figures, testing visuospatial reasoning.
TalkThe client speaks on a favorite topic, evaluating spontaneous language production and cognitive organization.

Scope and Limitations

EEG signals are complex and can be influenced by numerous factors (e.g., fatigue, emotional state, external distractions). While we aim to form an integrative picture of personality and cognitive style from this data, all interpretations should be considered suggestive rather than definitive. This information is not intended to replace professional psychological or medical evaluation, diagnosis, or treatment. If you have concerns about your mental health or well-being, please consult a qualified healthcare professional.

1. Your Brain's Five "Wave Types"

Brain regions can fire at different frequencies. We measure five key brainwave bands, from very slow to very fast, to understand the state of each region.

Delta

(0.5-4 Hz)

Very slow waves, often linked to deep, restorative sleep.

Theta

(4-8 Hz)

Slow waves associated with drowsiness, meditation, and memory.

Alpha

(8-12 Hz)

Intermediate waves, dominant during calm, wakeful relaxation.

Beta

(12-30 Hz)

Fast waves related to active thinking and focused attention.

Gamma

(30-100 Hz)

Very fast waves for high-level information processing.

2. Our Main Tool: The "z-score"

The z-score (activity score) is a simple measure that shows how much the activity (amplitude) of a brain region differs from the average across all regions. In other words, it tells you if a particular region is unusually active or less active compared to the rest of the brain, so it helps us spot which areas stand out. We calculate the z-score for per region and for each wavetype.

4. What It All Means: The Results

This comparison tells us if a brain region is "Balanced" or has "Specialized" characteristics.

Balanced Region

This means the area is working in a way that is very similar to the average. It’s operating smoothly and effectively within the normal range, just as we'd expect.

Specialized Region

This means the area works in a way that is different from the average. This brings strenghts and challanges with it. Here are the types of specialization:

Slowwaves I, II, III: Indicate increasing levels of under-arousal or cortical slowing.
High I, II, III: Suggest increasing levels of cortical over-arousal or a "mental overdrive" state.
Theta Dom I, II, III: Signify increasing dominance of Theta waves, linked to creativity but also inattention.
Alpha Dom I, II, III: Show increasing Alpha wave dominance, essential for calm focus but excess can lead to low mood.
